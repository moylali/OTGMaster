Container Master opens container files that are stored on your phone, so you can use the files inside them in your other apps.

<b>What it opens</b>
• VeraCrypt volumes made with VeraCrypt's defaults, AES and SHA-512, with or without a PIM
• LUKS1 and LUKS2 volumes with AES-XTS, including LUKS2 with Argon2id
• ZIP archives, stored or compressed, with no password
• ISO 9660 disc images, with Joliet names, with no password
• TAR archives, plain or gzip-compressed (.tar.gz)
• 7z archives, including ones encrypted with a password
• macOS disk images (.dmg) with APFS, exFAT, FAT32, NTFS or ext4 inside, compressed with zlib or bzip2 or not at all
• restic backup repositories: pick the folder, enter its password, and every snapshot opens as a folder
• AES Crypt files (version 2), checked whole before they open
• Inside an encrypted volume: exFAT, NTFS and ext4

<b>How it works</b>
Choose a file with the system picker, or open it from another app with "Open with", and enter its password. Its files then show up in the Files app and in any app that opens files, until you lock it. Lock one container, or Lock all from the notification. A container also locks itself after a time without use, 15 minutes unless you change it, and can lock when the screen turns off.

<b>Read-only by default</b>
Read-only mode is on unless you turn it off, and then the file you open stays exactly as it was. With it off, VeraCrypt and LUKS containers open for writing: add, change and delete the files inside them from any app. ZIP, ISO and AES Crypt files always open read-only.

<b>Private</b>
No network access, no ads, no tracking. Passwords are stored only if you turn on Remember passwords, and then only under your phone's lock: reading one back needs your fingerprint or screen lock. File names are masked in the issue report the app can send, and you see and edit everything before it is sent.

Container Master is made by the same project as OTG Master, which opens encrypted USB drives. It is free and open source (GPL-3.0-or-later).
